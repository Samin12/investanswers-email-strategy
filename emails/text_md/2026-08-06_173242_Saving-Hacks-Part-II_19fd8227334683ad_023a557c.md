 It has been a while since I update this and this note above inspired me. There are a lot of ways to cut this down. 

 Anyhow, also inspired by our member Timothee who mined the whole conversation going back months in the Savings Hacks channel. Here is a summary of many of the takeaways from Savings Hacks channel:

 Track every expense for a month or two, then build a budget around what you actually spend.

 Cancel any subscription you don’t regularly use (gym, streaming, software, etc.).

 Cook meals at home and brew your own coffee instead of dining out or cafe runs.

 Comparison-shop insurance and bundle internet, cable, and phone services to lower monthly bills.

 Always ask for – or hunt down – student, senior, or membership discounts before paying.

 Pay off high-interest debt first; refinance whenever you can lock in a lower rate.

 Make your home more efficient (seal drafts, install programmable thermostats) and consider downsizing or getting a roommate.

 Set up automatic transfers so money moves into savings or investments the moment you’re paid.

 Skip luxury brands and pricey vacations if “status” isn’t part of your real goals.

 Limit credit-card swipes to fixed bills (utilities, phone, basic groceries) to avoid overspending.

 Build a budget quickly: download the last 3 months of bank data, categorize every transaction, and total the columns.

 Judge entertainment by “hours per dollar”; board games and discounted video games beat most nights out.

 Enjoy cheap drinks: tall cans + mini liquor bottles, or learn to brew your own beer.

 Choose low-cost hobbies (reading, DIY projects) and skip frequent trips or weekend getaways.

 Let clearance stickers guide grocery choices and compare cost per pound, not package size.

 Buy kids’ toys second-hand (Marketplace, consignment shops) at roughly 80 % off retail.

 Store 80 % of kids’ toys away and rotate them monthly so everything feels “new” without buying more.

 Repair rather than replace big-ticket items (e.g., swap bearings in a washer, revive a thrift-store vacuum).

 Share streaming-service logins (where terms allow) and accept single-screen limits to cut monthly costs.
